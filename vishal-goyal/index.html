How I Think

Five things that shape how I work.

These aren't principles I wrote for a portfolio. They're patterns I've noticed in myself across every product I've built.

01

I think in ecosystems, not features.

A product doesn't exist in isolation. Before thinking about screens or functionality, I want to understand: who are all the actors? What happens before and after a user interacts with our product? Where does data originate, how does it move, and where does it get transformed? What can be simplified, automated, or improved? I'm interested in the entire workflow, not just the feature being built.

02

I care about what NOT to build.

In an era where new AI capabilities emerge constantly, it's easy to build something because it's possible. The harder questions: What problem are we actually solving? Is this a meaningful customer problem? Are we removing friction or adding complexity? Does AI genuinely improve the outcome? What's the simplest solution that creates real value? I've killed features that looked impressive and held the line on decisions others wanted to rush.

03

I like going deep.

I investigate beyond what's immediately required. This means understanding data models and relationships, API contracts, system dependencies, LLM behavior, and user analytics. The goal isn't technical complexity for its own sake — it's making better decisions and finding opportunities that remain invisible to those who only read the surface-level brief. When I joined Task Mining at ServiceNow, I personally tested OS-level signal capture before I wrote a single product requirement.

04

I am hands-on.

I explore products myself. I speak directly with customers. I test assumptions quickly using prototypes and AI coding tools. I don't see PM work as writing requirements and waiting for engineering. I work alongside design and engineering, iterating based on what I learn from real use — not from meeting notes.

05

I am comfortable with parallel ownership.

While one initiative moves toward delivery, I investigate the next opportunity, work through an integration dependency, or think about the next strategic bet. I operate well with ambiguity, ownership, and no established playbook. I don't need everything to be defined before I start working.

HireQuotient  ·  0→1 Product Build

Contacts — Building a Recruiter-First CRM from Zero

The gap I found

We gave recruiters talent intelligence, candidate sourcing, and outreach. But through user interviews, I realised we were only seeing part of the recruitment workflow. Recruiters were tracking everything that mattered — why a candidate was rejected, what happened in the interview, what feedback was pending, what teammates had done — in spreadsheets, side CRMs, and email threads. We were a point tool for sourcing, blind to what happened after.

The missing context wasn't just an inconvenience. It meant our AI intelligence was working with an incomplete picture of the recruitment process.

The product insight

The opportunity wasn't to improve sourcing — it was to capture the full recruitment workflow and make it visible inside the platform. If we understood what happened to candidates after sourcing, every other product capability — intelligence, matching, outreach — would become more valuable. We could move from a point tool to a workflow hub.

What I conceptualised and shipped

Contacts — a recruiter-first CRM within the platform. Core capabilities: contact list creation and management, candidate-recruiter interaction tracking, activity and recruitment progress tracking, centralised candidate records, and team collaboration around recruitment activity. The objective was not to increase session time — it was to capture missing workflow context that made everything else more intelligent.

HireQuotient  ·  Product Strategy + Build vs Buy

ATS Integrations — The Decision That Closed 20 Deals in 2 Weeks

The ask

Sales came to me with a list: 20 ATS integrations — Oracle, BambooHR, Greenhouse, and others. Customers were asking. Deals were contingent on them. The engineering estimate was 8 weeks. The pipeline wasn't going to wait 8 weeks.

The decision

Before committing to native builds, I went to sales leadership to understand what was actually blocking closures. The question I asked wasn't "how do we build 20 integrations?" — it was "which specific integrations are losing us deals, and what's the fastest credible path to supporting them?"

What I found: the demand pattern didn't require native implementations for each ATS. It required a unified integration layer. I evaluated a third-party ATS integration platform — one integration, 20+ connectors — and made the case internally. The build vs buy decision wasn't just faster; it was architecturally better for our scale and maintenance burden.

What I owned

Evaluating the integration platform, aligning with engineering on the tradeoffs, working with sales to define what a usable initial offering looked like, and driving the decision to market. I connected four functions — product, engineering, sales, and customers — into a single decision with a clear outcome.

ServiceNow  ·  Enterprise AI Product

Task Mining — From Raw Process Signals to Autonomous Agent Blueprints

The context

Task Mining captures raw OS-level signals — every click, keystroke, and application interaction — to understand how work actually happens in an enterprise. The broader opportunity was connecting this process intelligence with ServiceNow's Automation Center to build end-to-end agentic workflows. The ambition: an enterprise where work patterns are captured, understood, and automated without manual configuration.

The gap

Raw captured signals are not agent instructions. They tell you what happened — not how to replicate it in a way an AI agent can follow reliably. The gap between "here are the steps a user took" and "here is a structured instruction an agent can execute autonomously" required something to bridge them. I personally tested what signals were being captured at the OS level, identified where the gaps were between raw data and usable instructions, and worked with engineering and the Automation Center team on the integration design.

What shipped

An Automation Center–Task Mining integration that uses LLMs to auto-generate agent skeletons and detailed workflow instructions from process signal data. What previously required 20–40 minutes of manual expert authoring now happens in under 2 minutes, with ~90% output accuracy on the target workflows. This was the baseline integration required for agentic automation — showcased at ServiceNow Knowledge 2026.

Product Thinking

How I approach AI and technical depth.

Two areas where I think differently from most PMs: how I work with LLM products, and how I build technical understanding without being an engineer.

My approach to AI product management

I've worked on LLM-powered products since 2023. My working assumption is that LLMs are probabilistic systems, not deterministic software. The most common mistake I've seen is treating poor model output as a fine-tuning problem before investigating the actual causes.

Before jumping to model changes, I look at: input quality — what's actually being fed to the model; context availability — does it have what it needs to generate a useful response; instructions — are they specific, unambiguous, with examples; evaluation — how are we actually measuring quality; failure patterns — where does it break, and why.

The measure of success I care about: did the AI help someone make a better decision? Did it remove repetitive work? Did it prevent an error? Did it identify something previously missed? If the answer to all of those is no, the AI feature doesn't justify its complexity.

Integrations & APIs

REST APIs, authentication flows, data mapping between systems, synchronisation logic, error handling, and third-party integration tradeoffs. I understand why a webhook-based integration behaves differently from a polling one — and when each is appropriate.

Data & Systems

Data models, entity relationships, cross-product data flows, transformation logic, and system dependencies. When I work on an integration, I trace what happens to data end to end — not just at the point of connection.

AI & LLMs

Prompt design, context management, evaluation frameworks, human-in-the-loop workflows, and agentic automation. I understand precision vs recall tradeoffs in AI output and how to instrument for both.

Product Execution

Discovery, roadmapping, prioritisation frameworks, PRDs, UAT, analytics instrumentation, and adoption tracking. I treat each of these as a connected discipline, not a checklist.
